Saving your tooth is always the goal.

Root canal therapy removes infected or inflamed pulp tissue from inside the tooth — relieving pain, halting infection, and preserving the natural tooth that anchors your bite and smile for years to come.

Modern endodontic treatment is nothing like its reputation. With today's anesthesia, rotary instrumentation, and microscope-guided access, the vast majority of patients report little to no discomfort — and most are back to normal activity the same day.

Our specialists perform root canal therapy exclusively, completing thousands of cases each year across a full range of complexities, including curved canals, calcified anatomy, and previously treated teeth.

Step by Step

What to Expect

01

Consultation & Imaging

We begin with a detailed examination and, when needed, cone-beam CT imaging to map your tooth's unique anatomy in three dimensions before any treatment begins.

02

Comfortable Anesthesia

We take time to ensure you are fully numb and at ease. Sedation options are available for patients who prefer additional comfort or have dental anxiety.

03

Microscope-Guided Treatment

Using a dental operating microscope with up to 25× magnification, we access, clean, and shape each canal with precision invisible to the naked eye.

04

Sealing & Protection

Canals are filled with biocompatible material and sealed. We then coordinate with your dentist for the final restoration — typically a crown — to fully protect the tooth.

05

Follow-Up & Healing

Most patients return to normal activity within 24 hours. We monitor healing and remain available if any questions arise during your recovery.

Common Questions

Answered with honesty.

Does root canal therapy hurt?

With modern anesthesia, the procedure itself should not be painful. You may feel pressure during treatment but not sharp pain. Some sensitivity in the days following is normal and manageable with over-the-counter medication.

How many appointments will I need?

The majority of root canals are completed in a single appointment. More complex cases — severely curved canals, calcification, or active infections — may benefit from a second visit to ensure complete healing.

Is it better to extract and get an implant?

Nothing replaces your natural tooth. Root canal therapy preserves the original tooth root, maintaining the bone structure and bite function that implants approximate but cannot fully replicate. Saving your tooth is almost always the preferred option when possible.

What happens after treatment?

You will return to your general dentist for a permanent restoration, typically a crown, to protect the treated tooth. Until then, avoid chewing on that side. We follow up at 6 months and one year to confirm complete healing.

Do you accept my insurance?

We work with most major dental insurance plans and will help verify your coverage before treatment. Our team handles all insurance paperwork and can discuss flexible payment options for any remaining balance.
